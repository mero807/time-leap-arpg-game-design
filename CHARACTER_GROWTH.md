# Character Growth

## No Global Character Level

Current direction:

**The game does not use a standard global Character Level.**

Growth is primarily attached to the current job.

Example:

- Warrior Job Lv 21
- Mage Job Lv 14
- Assassin Job Lv 8

This creates a strong link between experience and job identity.

---

## Experience

Experience is gained through meaningful actions such as defeating enemies.

The world should not artificially distinguish only monsters as valid combat targets.

Human enemies, hostile faction members, bosses, and other combat encounters may also grant experience depending on context.

Exact XP formulas are unresolved.

---

## Stat Points

Current decision:

**Each Job Lv grants 1 stat point.**

Possible base stats:

- STR — physical power / heavy weapon affinity
- VIT — health / defense / resilience
- DEX — technique / precision / critical interaction
- AGI — mobility / speed / evasive interaction
- INT — magic / knowledge / status / summon affinity

These names and formulas are still candidates, not final.

---

## Job-Specific Stat State

Stat allocations should be associated with the job's progression state rather than becoming one infinitely accumulating global pool.

This avoids a player leveling 100 jobs and obtaining thousands of universal stat points.

Example:

- Warrior Job Lv 30 -> 30 Warrior stat points
- Mage Job Lv 20 -> 20 Mage stat points

Changing back to Warrior restores Warrior's stat configuration in the current timeline.

Time-leaping rewinds these values to their historical state.

---

## Hidden Stats

A major idea is that some stats do not exist visibly at the beginning.

They may unlock when unusual conditions are met.

Examples:

- STR 15 + INT 15 -> hidden hybrid attribute
- AGI 25 + DEX 25 -> reaction-related hidden stat
- high VIT while heavily cursed -> abnormal resistance stat

A hidden stat may:

- unlock a new mechanic
- modify combat behavior
- enable a hidden job
- reveal world interactions
- change NPC responses

This makes stat allocation itself a discovery system.

---

## Bad-Looking Builds Can Be Interesting

The design should resist creating one obvious "correct" stat distribution for every job.

An apparently inefficient build may reveal content.

Example:

Most Mage players stack INT.

A player who invests heavily in STR might discover:

- a hidden skill
- a melee magic mechanic
- a hidden job
- unusual NPC dialogue

This rewards experimentation.

---

## Respec

Respec rules are unresolved.

Possible options:

- free in safe areas
- item-based
- limited but repeatable
- unlocked through a specific NPC/job
- time-leap naturally rewinds to earlier allocations

Because hidden conditions depend on stat layouts, respec should not make experimentation unbearably expensive.

---

## Job Mastery as Knowledge

Whether a mastered job remains permanently marked as "experienced/mastered" after rewinding physical progression must be defined carefully.

Current direction favors preserving **knowledge/history flags** while rewinding actual levels.

Example:

The player once mastered Knight in a future that no longer exists.

After rewinding, they may retain:

- knowledge that Knight can be mastered
- a historical record that this mastery was experienced

But not necessarily:

- Knight Job Lv 50 right now
- Lv 50 stat points
- physical skills from that future

This supports time-based hidden conditions without letting future power trivialize the past.
