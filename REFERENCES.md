# References & Community Comparisons

This file collects games, stories, and design comparisons that may be useful when thinking about the project.

Some are direct design inspirations already mentioned in the repository. Others were suggested by people discussing the concept on Reddit.

**Being listed here does not mean the project is trying to copy that work.**  
A reference may be useful for one mechanic, one feeling, one design problem, or simply as a comparison that helps clarify what this project is trying to do differently.

---

## Existing Design Direction

### Hades II
Useful reference for:
- fast isometric action
- combat readability
- responsive movement
- satisfying moment-to-moment combat

### Monster Hunter
Useful reference for:
- strongly differentiated weapon identities
- readable enemy attacks
- learning enemy behavior through repeated encounters
- defensive timing and counterplay

### Skyrim
Useful reference for:
- freedom to ignore an intended heroic path
- allowing the player to exist in the world on their own terms
- exploration-driven discovery

### Cinderia
Useful reference for:
- broad build variety
- stylized character presentation

### Ruby Knight
Useful reference for:
- dark-fantasy atmosphere
- top-down / angled presentation direction

## Community-Suggested References

These were brought up during discussion of the Time Leap concept.

### Outer Wilds
Community comparison:
- knowledge itself functions as progression
- a sufficiently informed player can act very differently even without traditional permanent power growth

Why it is relevant here:
- this project also treats player knowledge as a persistent form of progression
- however, the idea here extends that knowledge into RPG systems such as hidden jobs, builds, NPC outcomes, world history, and timeline rewriting

### In Stars and Time
Community comparison:
- repeated loops and retained knowledge
- learning across repeated versions of events

Why it may be useful:
- another example of making repetition meaningful through accumulated understanding

### Twelve Minutes
Community suggestion:
- time-loop gameplay built around learning information across repeated attempts

Why it may be useful:
- useful for studying how information discovered in one loop changes decisions in another

### The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask
Community suggestion:
- time travel is part of the gameplay cycle rather than only a story device

Why it may be useful:
- useful for studying repeated schedules, revisiting earlier events, and designing a world around a controllable time structure

### Re:Zero
Community comparison:
- knowledge survives while events reset
- repeated histories can change how a character approaches the same situation

Important difference:
- this project is not intended to tie rewinding only to death or fixed save points
- the player should intentionally choose when and where to return within previously experienced history

### The Elder Scrolls II: Daggerfall
Mentioned in community discussion as a possible comparison point alongside procedural or large-scale RPG systems.

Potentially useful for:
- thinking about large systemic worlds
- emergent combinations of systems rather than only authored linear content


### Mother of Learning
Community suggestion:
- a fantasy time-loop story where the protagonist is trapped in a roughly one-month loop ending in a major disaster
- repeated loops turn accumulated knowledge and understanding into meaningful progress

Why it may be useful:
- useful for studying how repeated histories can gradually reveal the causes behind a disaster
- relevant to the idea that the player becomes more capable through knowledge rather than only permanent numerical growth

Important difference:
- this project is not intended to use one fixed repeating month as its central structure
- the player should be able to intentionally return to previously experienced points in history and create divergent futures

## Useful Comparisons That Are Not Design Goals

### Roguelikes / Roguelites
People naturally compare the concept to roguelikes because some forms of progression survive while a run or state resets.

Similarity:
- knowledge and player mastery can persist across resets

Difference:
- the core loop is not intended to be "start a new run"
- the player rewrites specific points in an ongoing history and then observes the resulting future

### Save Scumming
This comparison came up repeatedly in discussion.

Similarity:
- a player may return to an earlier state after seeing an undesirable outcome

Difference:
- rewinding is an explicit world mechanic with consequences
- changing the past can create new future states rather than merely restoring a save
- Causality Distortion can introduce entirely new content
- there may be no single objectively "best" outcome to reload toward

The intended fantasy is not:

> "Reload until the result is perfect."

It is closer to:

> **"Learn from one history, rewrite it, then discover what the new history became."**

## Community Design Lessons

The comparisons above are useful not only as references, but as warnings about what the project needs to communicate clearly.

### Knowledge must be more than quest answers

Persistent knowledge should include understanding:

- enemy behavior
- ecosystems
- NPC routines
- hidden interactions
- environmental clues
- world history
- unusual item uses
- hidden jobs and unlock conditions

The player should gradually understand **how the world works**, not just memorize solutions.

### Rewinding must create new possibilities

If time travel only functions like loading an older save, the mechanic loses much of its identity.

The design should instead emphasize:

- altered historical consequences
- new world states
- new discoveries
- causality anomalies
- encounters whose meaning changes once the player knows more

### Player mastery should matter

Even if stats and equipment rewind, the player should become more capable because they:

- recognize attack cues
- understand creature behavior
- notice clues they previously ignored
- know which events are connected
- can intentionally create different historical outcomes
